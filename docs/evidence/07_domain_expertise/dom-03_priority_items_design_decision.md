# DOM-03: Priority Items Design Decision

AED and LUCAS Device checks are surfaced as priority items at the top of every check because both pieces of equipment carry legal and protocol compliance requirements that supersede routine inventory — a missing or non-functional AED is a liability and regulatory issue, not just a stocking gap. Both devices are also time-sensitive by nature: they matter most in the first minutes of a cardiac arrest, so any battery, pad, or readiness failure needs to surface immediately rather than get buried among forty-plus compartment line items. Putting them first, before the compartment walk begins, means a responder catches a critical equipment failure in the first 30 seconds of a check instead of the last five minutes — early enough to still swap equipment or flag it before the shift starts. This ordering was a deliberate design decision, not an alphabetical or system-generated default: it reflects the actual risk hierarchy of what's on the vehicle, prioritizing life-safety-critical devices over general supply counts.
