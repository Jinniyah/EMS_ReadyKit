# WP-06: AI-Partnered Development

I don't think a solo developer could have built an application this complex — 13 routers, 29 migrations, 763 tests, full Azure AD integration, and a multi-role PWA — in roughly 170 hours without AI assistance, and I'd rather say that plainly than let the hour count imply otherwise. I partnered with Claude throughout the build: every session started by having it read `CLAUDE.md` and `CODEBASE_INDEX.md` so it picked up full project context instead of starting cold, and every session ended with backlog discipline — completed items moved out of `backlog.md` and written up as a session entry in `backlog_completed.md` — so the project stayed navigable across 43 sessions instead of turning into an undocumented pile of changes.

The division of labor was deliberate, not incidental. I made the domain calls — what counts as a priority item, how per-station item uniqueness should work, what Earl needs to see versus what a responder needs to see, when a design decision needed to be a backlog item versus an immediate fix. Claude did the execution at a pace and consistency I couldn't match alone: reading model, schema, and router together before any edit; writing and running the test suite; catching cross-file dependencies I would have missed at hour eleven of my own working session. That combination — my judgment and domain expertise directing the work, AI assistance executing it thoroughly and consistently — is what made the scope realistic in the time available, and it's a workflow I'd defend in an interview as a legitimate professional skill, not a shortcut.

The part of this process AI couldn't do for me was user testing and feedback, and that's where I put a deliberate, disproportionate share of my own time. Speed in generating code doesn't mean anything if the resulting app doesn't hold up against a real crew under real conditions, so I ran structured UAT with the actual Newberg Township EMS team — role-specific test cases for Administrator, Supervisor, and Responder — and treated every finding as a root-cause question before touching code, not just a bug to patch over. That feedback loop, not the AI partnership, is what turned a fast-built app into one I'd trust a real EMS team to rely on: AI helped me build quickly, but the responders are the ones who told me whether I'd built the right thing.
